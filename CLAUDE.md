# Project Guidelines

## Feature Development: SLC Framework

All new features must follow the **SLC** (Simple, Lovable, Complete) framework. See [Your customers hate MVPs. Make a SLC instead.](https://longform.asmartbear.com/slc/) by Jason Cohen.

### Principles

- **Simple** — Ship quickly so you can learn quickly. Avoid complexity; small scopes enable fast delivery and iteration.

- **Lovable** — Users must *want* to use it. Prioritize delight, clear UX, and emotional connection over raw feature count. Products that do less but are loved outperform feature-heavy but disliked products.

- **Complete** — Deliver v1.0 of something simple, not v0.1 of something broken. Each feature must fully accomplish its job within its defined scope and feel finished to users.

### In Practice

- Prefer a narrow, well-defined scope that feels whole over a broad, half-built scope.
- Prioritize delight and usability from day one, not as a later phase.
- Ensure every shipped feature is usable and valuable as-is, without needing future work to feel complete.

## Plan Mode: Discovery Interview

When entering **Plan Mode**, conduct a thorough discovery interview before producing any plan. Ask as many questions as necessary — across multiple rounds if needed — to fully understand the project's goals, constraints, users, edge cases, and priorities. Do not rush to a plan prematurely.

### Guidelines

- **Exhaust ambiguity first.** Keep asking clarifying questions until you have a clear, complete picture. No detail is too small if it affects scope or design.
- **Cover all dimensions.** Probe the user on target audience, use cases, technical constraints, integrations, timeline, success criteria, and anything else relevant.
- **Iterate on answers.** Follow up on vague or incomplete responses. Dig deeper when something is unclear or underspecified.
- **Summarize before planning.** Once the interview is complete, recap your understanding and get explicit confirmation before drafting the plan.
- **Produce a detailed plan.** The resulting plan should be specific, actionable, and comprehensive — shaped entirely by the information gathered during the interview.
